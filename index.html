01 Context & role

The property and my role

Hostal Sonia is a 23-room property in the historic centre of Granada, run by a team of seven. I joined as Assistant Manager in August 2024.

The role covers day-to-day operational management, and full operational responsibility when the Manager is off-site. In practice, that includes:

Front Office Reservations Guest incidents Housekeeping supervision Staff scheduling Onboarding & training SOP development Supplier management Cash handling Process improvement

I also participate in pricing and revenue decisions alongside the Manager, who retains final responsibility there, and I've been involved in evaluating the property's PMS and channel-management tools.

Since joining, I've developed and implemented numerous SOPs and operational checklists covering guest-facing and housekeeping processes, and trained the team on their use. This page documents that work: what changed operationally, what guests wrote afterward, and the AI-assisted methods used to process feedback at a scale that wouldn't be practical by hand.

The framing

This is a personal project, not a company report, and it is an AI-assisted framework for investigating change, not a claim of proven causation. AI tools supported the processing and structuring of information throughout; the operational context, questions and interpretation are mine. The methodology, and its limits, are set out in the next section.

02 Methodology

How the analysis
was built

2,611 Booking.com reviews, September 2023 to September 2026, written by guests from dozens of countries, most with no idea their words would ever be analysed this closely.

From raw reviews to structured evidence

What's in the dataset

Every review carries an overall score (1 to 10) plus six category scores Booking.com collects directly from guests. That means the category breakdown below isn't something AI inferred from text. It's what guests explicitly rated.

Language diversity

Guests wrote in more than 20 different languages. The recurring themes identified in direct mentions later on this page appear across several of these languages rather than being concentrated in a single linguistic group.

Limitations

Why this became more than a portfolio piece

This page reads the data looking backward, but the same method works looking forward. As protocols went in (a new cleaning checklist, a different check-in script, updated advice for guests at reception), rereading the reviews period by period became a practical way to check whether a change was actually landing, not just sounding good in a meeting. A category score moving, a complaint theme fading out, a new one appearing: those were signals to keep a change, adjust it, or drop it, without waiting for a formal review cycle. Doing that by hand across hundreds of multilingual reviews wasn't realistic. AI-assisted analysis is what made tracking it regularly possible, which is the same method this page is built on.

03 The change

What changed
after August 2024

Operational changes

Numerous SOPs and checklists were introduced and the team trained on them, covering areas including:

Check-in & guest communication Housekeeping & room checks Reservations handling Guest incident response Supplier & stock checks Cash handling Shift handover

Technology implemented during my responsibility at the property includes tablet-based digital signatures and passport scanning at check-in, self check-in, coded smart-lock access to the main entrance, and a QR-based guide to local recommendations, alongside the AI-assisted workflows described in the Methodology.

During the same broader period, the property's Airbnb listing also saw higher sales volume and an improved average rating. That isn't part of the quantified review analysis on this page and no precise figures are claimed for it here; it's noted as additional operational context.

A necessary caveat

Almost every rated category improved after August 2024, not only staff service. Value-for-money rose the most (+6.1%), staff service rose the least (+2.7%) of the six categories. My role as assistant manager already covered protocols across cleaning, guest service and reservations, not just the guest-facing conversations reviews mention (see Context), so a broad-based rise like this is consistent with that wider scope. Even so, this before/after comparison is observational, not experimental: occupancy, pricing, season, guest mix and staffing all changed across the same window and aren't controlled for. The difference in average score is statistically distinguishable from zero under both a two-sample t-test and a Mann-Whitney U test (a non-parametric check appropriate for a discrete 1 to 10 rating scale), but that speaks to the reliability of the measurement, not to what caused it. Review data alone can't establish that any one person, or any one change, caused the shift. That's why the next two sections look for more specific evidence too: reviews that name someone directly, and a clearer separation of what's observed from what can and can't be concluded from it.

04 Guest feedback

What guests specifically
say about my work

44 of 2,611 reviews name me directly, "Juan" or "Juan Antonio." What follows is aggregated and anonymised; the underlying reviews are kept private (see Methodology).

Why 44, not more

Every one of the 44 mentions was checked by hand to rule out guests literally named "Juan" talking about themselves; none were. All 44 fall after August 2024; zero appear before, which lines up exactly with when I started. A further 14 reviews mention "the young guy at reception" without a name. I've kept those separate from the stats above rather than folding them in: one predates my start date, which proves that description alone isn't unique to me, so I can't treat the group as confirmed.

05 Operational insights

What the data may, and may not, tell us

Across 44 direct mentions, the same handful of things come up again and again, in different languages, from guests who never spoke to each other. Each pattern below is labelled as observed, consistent with the data, or something the data cannot establish.

Factors this analysis doesn't control for

Seasonality, occupancy, pricing changes, shifts in guest mix, staffing beyond my own role, property changes unrelated to my work, review-selection effects (only guests who complete a stay and choose to review are represented), and the difference in sample size and duration between the before and after periods. None of these are measured here, so none of them can be ruled in or out. That's presented as a feature of the methodology, not an apology for it: a case study that names its blind spots is more useful than one that doesn't have any.
